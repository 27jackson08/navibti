Two domain facts worth encoding

Medical clearance is not required to return to school. It is required for Sport Step 4+. Most tools blur this and end up over-restricting. Stating the asymmetry correctly is a cheap, strong signal of domain knowledge.

Strict rest is out. Relative rest caps at 24–48 hours, and "a complete absence from the school environment for more than one week is not generally recommended." So the engine must also detect under-exposure and nudge upward. Every competing pacing app only ever caps. This is a genuine differentiator — take it.

02

Two engines, deliberately separated

The safety architecture is the split itself. A deterministic stage machine encodes the published protocols verbatim and decides what is permitted. A personalized Bayesian model decides how much, but only inside the box the guideline already drew. The model can never advance a stage.

03

What the model actually predicts

Because the guideline threshold is defined against the pre-activity value, the target variable is Δ — the worst within-day symptom increase over baseline, on the same 0–10 scale the guideline uses, plus how long it lasted. Two check-in questions carry the whole model, which is also what keeps the check-in under a minute.

Bayesian linear regression over five load domains — cognitive, visual-vestibular, physical, sleep/fatigue, emotional-autonomic — with a Normal–Inverse-Gamma conjugate prior. Closed-form nightly updates. No sampling, no training loop, no Python service: it is roughly 150 lines of TypeScript that runs in-process and unit-tests alongside everything else.

Tolerance for a domain is the largest dose where the posterior predictive still satisfies P(Δ > 2) ≤ 0.2.

Say this part out loud in the demo

Sparse data produces a wide posterior, which automatically produces a lower recommended dose. The "be conservative when you don't know the user yet" requirement falls out of the math rather than being bolted on as a special case. That is the difference between a model and a heuristic with a model-shaped wrapper.

Explaining a flare without overclaiming

Contribution per domain is the posterior mean weight times today's exposure, ranked, delivered with a counterfactual: "most consistent with 3 back-to-back calls on 5 hours of sleep — had meetings been 2 instead of 5, predicted increase would be 1.2 rather than 3.4."

Then the part that wins the safety criterion: if two domains are strongly collinear in this user's own history, refuse to name a single cause. Say they moved together and the data can't separate them yet. Knowing when your model can't answer is worth more to these judges than a confident answer.

04

The packets — and the constraint that makes them safe

Accommodations come from a curated library, not free generation. Each item is a record carrying its domain, dose band, applicable stage range, target role, and citation. Composition is a deterministic query. An LLM may rephrase for tone, behind a validator that rejects any output introducing a recommendation absent from the selected set.

The model can rewrite a sentence. It cannot invent a clinical claim. That single constraint deserves its own slide.

05

Safety layer

Hard rules, enforced in one module, unit-tested like any other invariant.

  • Red flags — neck pain or tenderness, double vision, limb weakness or tingling, severe or worsening headache, seizure, loss of or deteriorating consciousness, repeated vomiting, growing agitation → full-screen interrupt, emergency-care instruction, no plan generated at all.
  • Never clears sport. Step 4+ requires written medical clearance; the app displays the requirement and declines to advance.
  • Never gates school on clearance — it states the opposite, correctly.
  • Prolonged symptoms — past 14 days in adults, 4 weeks in children → clinician-escalation banner citing the Living Guidelines.
  • Under three days of data → population prior only, labeled provisional, visibly wide margins.
  • Every artifact carries not-medical-advice framing, its citation, generation date, and current stage.
  • Share links are role-scoped to minimum necessary data, expiring, one-click revocable, access-logged. The patient chooses whether tolerance bands travel without raw symptom detail.
06

Measure the model, don't just ship it

No human data exists and none should be collected in eleven days. Instead, generate 21-day trajectories from known ground-truth coefficients. This converts "we built a model" into "we measured our model," which is the gap between a 3 and a 5 on technical complexity.

07

Accessibility is the product, not the polish

The users are photophobic and cognitively fatigued. Treat every item here as a functional requirement.

  • Low stimulation by default — no pure white, no pure black, nothing saturated. A warm dimmed mode plus user brightness and warmth controls.
  • Name the real tension. WCAG pushes contrast up; photophobia pushes glare down. Resolve it by reducing luminance rather than contrast, and be ready to explain exactly that — it is the best possible answer to an accessibility judging question.
  • No motion by default. Honor prefers-reduced-motion, no autoplay, no parallax.
  • Audio-first check-in — spoken prompts and voice input, so the daily log can be done with eyes closed.
  • Minimal typing — dials, steppers, large targets. Resumable if someone has to stop partway.
  • Adjustable text size, capped line length, optional dyslexia-friendly face, full keyboard navigation, screen-reader pass.

09

What to claim, and what not to

Claim: the first closed-loop accommodation translator built on personalized tolerance signals.

Do not claim the first personalized pacing app — MyBrainPacer and Parkwood hold that, and a sponsor-side judge knows it. Do not claim clinical validation. State plainly: evaluated on synthetic cohorts with known ground truth, not validated in humans, not a medical device.

Open the demo on the stakeholder, not the app. Show the email from the school demanding full attendance. Then show the packet. A judge should see the gap inside twenty seconds.
